How SPEADE works

The six steps

  1. Add PDFs… — they appear under Waiting to process.
  2. Process PDFs — the app reads scans (OCR) and adds accessibility tags. Already-processed files are skipped automatically.
  3. Review — click a document. The left side lists every heading, paragraph, list, and image; the right side shows the pages with a box around each tagged part. Click either side to highlight the other.
  4. Title & language — set what a screen reader should announce, then save.
  5. Decide — Approve (moves to the approved folder, done) or Reject (moves to rejected).
  6. Fix and return — repair rejected PDFs in Adobe Acrobat Pro, save over the same file, come back and Approve. Not approved = not done.

What to check when reviewing

  • Do the headings in the list match the document's real headings?
  • Does the top-to-bottom order match sensible reading order?
  • Are lists and tables tagged as lists and tables?
  • Does every meaningful image say Image — and have a description? ("needs description" means you write one, below the pages.)
  • Is any visible content missing a box entirely? No box = not tagged.

Fixing tags without leaving the app

  • One tag — click it (tree or page box): change its type, write an image description, mark it decorative, remove a wrapper, or move it earlier/later in the reading order.
  • Many tags — drag a rectangle on a page, like Acrobat. The bar that appears can merge them into one tag (the fix for over-fragmented text), change all, or mark all decorative.
  • Part of a tag — drag over just some of its lines and New tag from lines carves them out into their own tag — the fix for a heading swallowed into the top of a paragraph.
  • Untagged content — anything with no box at all (missed text, or something marked decorative) shows a dashed red highlight when you drag over it; Tag untagged gives it a brand-new tag. Decorative is therefore reversible.
  • Undo — Undo last change steps back one edit at a time; Undo all edits starts over from the original. Every edit re-runs the automatic check and is recorded in History.
  • Still Acrobat's job: selecting part of a single line and table header cells.

Common questions

Why did it skip some files when I pressed Process?
They were already processed and haven't changed. Replace the file in the input folder with a new version to process it again.
What does "Edited since processing" mean?
The file changed after the app made it — normally because you fixed it in Acrobat. That's expected; approving records the file as it is now.
A document says it is password-protected or damaged.
The app never guesses passwords and can't repair broken files. Ask the document's owner for a usable copy and add it again.
What is the "automatic check"?
veraPDF, the official PDF/UA accessibility validator. Its verdict is advice — your Approve/Reject is the decision, and it is recorded.
Who writes the image descriptions?
You do — select the image and type it below the pages (or in Acrobat). Descriptions are never machine-written. That's deliberate: quality needs a human.
Where do finished documents go?
outbox\approved — share the PDF only; the app's record files stay behind.

The three rules

  1. Originals are sacred — corrections happen on the draft, never the source file.
  2. Nothing ships without a human — only your Approve makes it accessible.
  3. When unsure, reject and ask — the queue can wait; students relying on the document can't.
